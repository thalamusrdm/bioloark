# Store experience playbook

Use these as selectable patterns, not a mandatory page template.

## Homepage

Keep the cinematic hero, then move quickly into one guided decision. Strong options:

- “מה מתאים לחלל שלי?” with choices for low light, bright indirect light, desk/shelf, statement piece, and low maintenance.
- Two intent paths: “לבחור יצירה מוכנה” and “לתכנן מערכת בהתאמה אישית.”
- A curated product row that explains why each item was selected, not only title and price.

Follow with proof close to the first purchase decision: verified pickup/delivery area, care support, craftsmanship, or warranty. A later editorial chapter can tell the studio story. Keep services visible, but separate consumer shopping from B2B consultation so neither path feels accidental.

## Collection pages

- Provide useful filters before ornamental sorting: product type, light, care level, size, price, availability, and suitability for beginners when the data exists.
- Show active filters, result count, clear reset, and an informative empty state.
- Cards should expose image, name, price, availability, one decision attribute, and a direct action or obvious product link.
- Add a short collection guide, but never bury products beneath a long manifesto.

## Product pages

Above the fold should contain: gallery, product name, price, availability, concise outcome, variant selection, primary add-to-cart, and delivery/pickup summary.

Then answer:

- What arrives and what is included?
- What size is it, and how does it look in a real space?
- Which light, humidity, watering, and skill level fit it?
- Is each piece unique, and how may it vary from the photo?
- How is a living product packed, delivered, supported, or guaranteed?

Use accordions or structured blocks for care, materials, delivery, and FAQ. Cross-sell only genuinely complementary items: light, tools, substrate, moss, or care accessories.

## Guided discovery

A Bioloark advisor should feel curatorial rather than gimmicky. Ask no more than needed to produce a useful result. Good dimensions are space, available light, desired presence, maintenance appetite, budget, and whether the visitor wants a finished work or to build one.

Return a small set of recommendations with a plain-language reason for each and a route to personal consultation when no product fits.

## Trust and proof

Place proof next to the anxiety it resolves:

- Near product cards: stock and price clarity.
- Near add-to-cart: delivery, live-arrival handling, returns/warranty, and care support.
- Near custom projects: process, timeline ranges, authentic case studies, and a specific consultation CTA.
- Near claims: verified counts, named sources, or no number at all.

Never invent reviews. Generic testimonials currently in code should be treated as provisional until the owner confirms they are authentic.

## Interaction language

- Primary shopping CTA: “לבחירת היצירה”, “הוספה לסל”, or “לצפייה בפרטים”, according to the actual action.
- Consultation CTA: “לתיאום שיחת היכרות” or “ספרו לנו על החלל”.
- Unavailable: “אזל כרגע — אפשר לעדכן אותי כשחוזר” only if notification exists; otherwise “אזל כרגע”.
- Empty cart: suggest one clear route back, then a small curated entry point.
- Search empty state: echo the query, suggest category shortcuts, and offer help.

## Responsive and accessibility checks

- Test at approximately 390px and 1280px widths.
- Make mobile CTA and cart controls thumb-reachable and at least 44px in target size.
- Verify RTL order for grids, arrows, prices, Latin names, breadcrumbs, and carousels.
- Maintain readable contrast over images after motion settles; avoid entrance states that temporarily make core content unreadable.
- Preserve document order, heading hierarchy, focus visibility, Escape behavior for overlays, and reduced motion.

## Visual quality bar

Avoid generic card oceans, excessive rounded rectangles, random icon sets, and simultaneous promotion bars/popups/chat widgets. One immersive hero, one guided choice, one proof language, and one coherent CTA hierarchy will feel more premium than many competing devices.
